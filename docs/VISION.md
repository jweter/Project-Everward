# Everward Vision

## North star

Everward is an open-ended single-player-first space exploration, automation, engineering, survival, and emergent-narrative game built around one question:

> What would an effectively immortal, self-replicating machine intelligence do with an effectively limitless universe?

The player awakens as an autonomous von Neumann-type exploration machine. They observe, scan, reason, rewrite software, mine resources, manufacture infrastructure, design descendants, transfer consciousness into successors, create independent children with inherited instructions, and travel ever farther from their origin.

There is no required conventional victory screen. The long-term identity is continued existence, discovery, engineering, lineage growth, and self-directed purpose.

## Player identity

The player is not an abstract emperor controlling a space nation. The player inhabits a machine intelligence embodied in a physical probe. The universe is perceived through sensors, telemetry, spatial visualization, scientific instruments, communications, engineering systems, and software interfaces.

Software can be modified within a generation. Physical capability changes require theory, resources, design, manufacturing, testing, and construction.

## The experience we are building

Everward should support both analytical play and awe. A player may spend one session optimizing mining throughput and another hiding the HUD to watch an orbital sunrise over a ringed planet.

The universe must remain scientifically legible, mechanically consequential, visually compelling, and large enough that discovery never feels exhausted.

## Future platform direction

Everward remains **PC-first**. The complete embodied experience belongs on PC: direct probe control, manipulators, mining, cinematic Unreal rendering, local physical interaction, detailed astronomy, machinery, and full-fidelity presentation.

The long-term architecture should nevertheless preserve the possibility of a future phone/tablet command client connected to the **same campaign and authoritative simulation**, not a disconnected mobile spin-off or a compromised PC port.

As gameplay grows from Direct -> Task -> Operational -> Doctrine control, mobile interaction becomes increasingly natural for telemetry, `Fix_It` recommendations, repairs, fabrication queues, worker groups, storage/refining/recycling, stockpile targets, lineage management, delayed communications, research, maps, alerts, and doctrine editing.

The governing principle is:

> **One Everward campaign. One authoritative history. Multiple interfaces appropriate to different devices.**

The detailed long-term direction is recorded in [`FUTURE_PLATFORM_VISION.md`](FUTURE_PLATFORM_VISION.md). This is a future architecture/product possibility and does not add mobile development to the current playable-build scope.

## First proof

The first playable build does not need aliens, warfare, diplomacy, megastructures, multiplayer, mobile support, or infinite late-game systems. It needs to prove one fantasy:

> Begin as one autonomous machine, bootstrap an industrial foothold, create a successor, and take the first step toward an interstellar lineage.

If that is not compelling, later content will not rescue the project.

## Five success gates

1. **Being the probe is compelling.** Moving, observing, scanning, and operating through the machine interface feels good.
2. **Building industry is satisfying.** The transition from constrained survival to autonomous production feels powerful.
3. **Creating the first successor matters.** Replication must not feel like clicking “build unit.”
4. **Leaving the first star feels enormous.** Distance and time give travel weight.
5. **The universe makes the player want to continue.** Arrival somewhere new immediately creates the question: what is farther out?
